- **Sources (accessed 7 October 2026):**
  - J. Faryś, "Józef Piłsudski w systemie polskiej demokracji 1922 r.", 2021, pp. 72–76, citing the Sejm stenographic reports (https://czasopisma.ipn.gov.pl/index.php/pis/article/download/1035/811/845): the vote on Śliwiński of 7 July 1922, 201 against and 195 for with 3 abstentions; the left, the centre-left and the minorities against the right bloc, with the 16 conservatives of KPK deciding; Korfanty designated by the Main Commission on 14 July (219:206) and dropped; Nowak agreed by Piłsudski with the KPK and Piast leaders, proposed on Rataj's motion against the protesting right, investiture on 3 August 1922.
  - dzieje.pl, "100 lat temu urząd premiera objął Artur Śliwiński" (https://dzieje.pl/wiadomosci/100-lat-temu-urzad-premiera-objal-artur-sliwinski): Śliwiński as Piłsudski's own candidate, appointed 28 June 1922, brought down 205:195.
  - Wikipedia (pl), "Drugi rząd Antoniego Ponikowskiego", "Rząd Artura Śliwińskiego", "Rząd Juliana Nowaka", "Pierwszy rząd Władysława Sikorskiego", "Drugi rząd Władysława Grabskiego", "Rząd Aleksandra Skrzyńskiego" (https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drugi_rząd_Antoniego_Ponikowskiego and the pages of the same names): Ponikowski backed by 247 deputies and resigning on 6 June 1922; Nowak appointed 31 July and resigning 14 December 1922; Sikorski's cabinet resigning on 26 May 1923 after the Lanckorona Pact; the members of the Grabski and Skrzyński cabinets.
  - J. Goclon, Przegląd Nauk Historycznych 2013 (https://www.czasopisma.uni.lodz.pl/pnh/article/download/25472/25352/63792): Darowski's mission given up on 16 December 1922; Sikorski entrusted by Rataj; for Sikorski the socialists, the peasant deputies, NPR and the Slavic minorities, against him the National Democrats, the communists and the Jewish deputies.
  - W. Grabski, *Dwa lata pracy u podstaw państwowości naszej* (1927; https://wolnelektury.pl/media/book/txt/dwa-lata-pracy-u-podstaw-panstwowosci-naszej.txt) and dzieje.pl, "95 lat temu powołano drugi rząd Władysława Grabskiego" (https://dzieje.pl/aktualnosci/95-lat-temu-powolano-drugi-rzad-wladyslawa-grabskiego): on 21 December 1923 the Sejm took note of his statement 194:76; the centre and the right for, the left absent, the minorities and the communists against.
  - J. Chojnowski, Przegląd Historyczny 1976, t. 67, nr 4, pp. 593–616 (https://bazhum.muzhp.pl/media/texts/przeglad-historyczny/1976-tom-67-numer-4/przeglad_historyczny-r1976-t67-n4-s593-616.pdf), and histmag.org on Grabski's second cabinet (https://histmag.org/Rzad-ostatniej-szansy-rzad-sukcesu-drugi-rzad-Wladyslawa-Grabskiego-8838): the minorities and Grabski.
  - dzieje.pl, "Aleksander Skrzyński (1882–1931)" (https://dzieje.pl/postacie/aleksander-skrzynski-1882-1931): the coalition of ZLN, ChD, NPR, PSL Piast and PPS; PPS leaving on 20 April 1926.
  - For the PPS currents: the sources of the October 2026 research on the factions (Gołota; Kowalski, https://repozytorium.uni.wroc.pl/Content/107842/PDF/06_Kowalski_W_Stanowisko_Polskiej_Partii_Socjalistycznej_wobec_zamachu_majowego_w_1926_roku.pdf): Moraczewski as an open Piłsudskiite of the PPS right; Czapiński's place in the left uncertain.
- **Historical fact:** the order of the cabinets and their dates; Śliwiński brought down by the right bloc with KPK; Sikorski entrusted only after the assassination of President Narutowicz; the stances to Śliwiński, Sikorski and Grabski listed above. No evidence was found that Daszyński, Moraczewski, Thugutt, Witos or Rataj was proposed as prime minister in 1922.
- **Gameplay simplification (P):** the periods of the candidates as month windows (Śliwiński from January 1922 in an alternate early fall of Ponikowski); the values 80/50/20 and the rule that an opponent refuses and votes against; the neutral stance of the right to Nowak; Thugutt as a possible leader of the centre-left and the broad centre; the faction candidates of PPS, the 30% threshold and the effects ±5 and +5/−5/+3; Nowak standing beside Śliwiński in June–July 1922 as a player's alternative, described as a compromise of the Sejm (decision 2B of 8 October 2026; historically he was agreed by Piłsudski only after Śliwiński fell and Korfanty's designation failed); Skrzyński leading a non-party cabinet when the head of state forms one without PPS.
- **Confidence:** high for the dates and the Śliwiński and Grabski votes; medium for the stances to Sikorski (one secondary work, no numbers); low for the clubs' votes on Nowak. **Unresolved:** the club-by-club roll call on Śliwiński (sittings 324–326); the right's vote on Nowak (an unverified count of 193:139); Czapiński's faction; the votes of the Ukrainian, Belarusian and German clubs — TBD, historical research required.
- **Game use:** `PolishGovernment` (`CANDIDATES`, `EXPERT_STANCES`, `expertPeriodStatus`, `leaderStatus`, `candidateOptions`, `premierPart`, `npcEntries`).

## PL-1922-ELECTION-MINORITY-VOTES — the Sejm of 1922 and the cabinet resting on minority votes

- **Authority:** on 7 October 2026 the user asked why forming a cabinet after the 1922 election was so easy and for historical research; decisions 1A and 3A (technical reference 0.67, sections 8.8 and 23.40). The user's Notion workspace held no notes on the game.
- **Sources (accessed 7 October 2026):**
  - Seats of the election of 5 November 1922 (444): ChZJN 163, PSL Piast 70, Bloc of National Minorities 66, PSL Wyzwolenie 49, PPS 41, NPR 18, other Jewish and Ukrainian lists about 16–17, Polish Centre 6, the communist list 2, others 4 — Wikipedia (pl), "Wybory parlamentarne w Polsce w 1922 roku" (https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wybory_parlamentarne_w_Polsce_w_1922_roku); vote shares of the right about 29%, the centre 24%, the left 25%, the minority bloc 22%: IPN, "Czasy sejmokracji" (https://przystanekhistoria.pl/pa2/tematy/gabriel-narutowicz/98308,Czasy-sejmokracji.pdf).
  - Narutowicz was elected president on 9 December 1922 with 289 votes against 227, with the left, PSL Piast and the national minorities; part of the right-wing press called him "the Jewish president" — Wikipedia (en), "Gabriel Narutowicz" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gabriel_Narutowicz); M. Przeperski, "Ku śmierci prezydenta" (https://teologiapolityczna.pl/michal-przeperski-ku-smierci-prezydenta-klimat-polityczny-jesienia-1922-r/pdf).
  - The Lanckorona Pact of 17 May 1923 between PSL Piast and the parties of ChZJN (stricter polonisation, a larger role of the Church) dismissed Sikorski's cabinet and formed Witos's Chjeno-Piast cabinet on 28 May 1923 — Wikipedia (en), "Lanckorona Pact" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lanckorona_Pact) and "Chjeno-Piast" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chjeno-Piast).
- **Historical fact:** arithmetically the left, PSL Piast and the minorities had a majority (the presidential vote of December 1922), but PPS formed no cabinet between 1922 and 1925 and PSL Piast turned to the right in 1923 (with `PL-1922-1926-CABINETS`).
- **Gameplay simplification (P):** the threshold of 185 MPs of a cabinet's own clubs after the election, and the rule that PSL Piast, NPR, PSChD and ZLN vote against a cabinet without Piast that passes only with the minorities, are game rules inferred from this background; they do not reconstruct a recorded vote. The 185 is calibrated on the test campaigns (175–181 MPs for the Centre-left, 185–186 with a joint list and a campaign).
- **Confidence:** high for the seat counts and the dates (several concordant secondary sources; the official statistics were not checked); the reaction of the clubs is a design inference. **Unresolved:** the exact club composition after the election and each club's votes on the cabinets of 1922–1923 — TBD, historical research required.
- **Game use:** `PolishGovernment.forecast`, `configurationStatus` (`MINORITY_VOTE_OPPONENTS`, `POST_ELECTION_OWN_SEATS`).

On 26 VII the Sejm rejected a no-confidence motion against the Naczelnik; on 29 VII Korfanty’s designation was withdrawn and Nowak accepted; his cabinet was appointed on 31 VII.

Piast and the Christian Democrats signed with ZLN the Lanckorona pact (17 V 1923): a “Polish majority”, a cabinet of Poles only, numerus clausus, settlement in the east (medium).
